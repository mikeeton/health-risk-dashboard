# Reference Audit

Date: 19 August 2026

## Scope and method

The reference list and all newly introduced citations were checked against publisher, DOI, PubMed, standards-body or government pages. Harvard author-date presentation was retained. Older sources were kept where they are foundational, define the dataset or document an established method. No reference was added solely to make the bibliography appear longer.

## Existing references retained

- Ancker et al. (2017): retained as directly relevant evidence on repeated alerts, workload and alert fatigue.
- Brooke (1996): retained as the foundational source for the System Usability Scale.
- Collins et al. (2024): retained as the current TRIPOD+AI reporting statement and a central methodological source.
- Dunn, Runge and Snyder (2018): retained as a foundational review of wearable biosensors and validation challenges.
- FDA, Health Canada and MHRA (2021): retained as official Good Machine Learning Practice principles.
- Goldberger et al. (2000): retained as the foundational PhysioNet resource citation.
- HL7 International (2019): retained as the official FHIR Release 4 specification.
- Kelly et al. (2019): retained for the clinical-translation gap in health AI.
- Liu et al. (2020): retained as the CONSORT-AI trial-reporting extension.
- Lundberg and Lee (2017): retained as the original SHAP paper.
- Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana (2005): retained as foundational probability-calibration research.
- Nielsen (1994): retained as a foundational usability source.
- Noah et al. (2018): retained as an established remote-monitoring meta-analysis.
- Rajkomar, Dean and Kohane (2019): retained as a broad peer-reviewed account of machine learning in medicine.

- Topol (2019): retained for the augmentation rather than replacement argument.

## New references added

- Ghassemi, M., Oakden-Rayner, L. and Beam, A.L. (2021), “The false hope of current approaches to explainable artificial intelligence in health care”, *The Lancet Digital Health*, 3(11), e745–e750. Added to provide a recent critical counterpoint to purely optimistic accounts of SHAP and explainability. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00208-9
- Lee, P., Bubeck, S. and Petro, J. (2023), “Benefits, limits, and risks of GPT-4 as an AI chatbot for medicine”, *New England Journal of Medicine*, 388(13), 1233–1239. Added to distinguish language-model capability from evidence of safe clinical use. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsr2214184
- Lu, J.K., Sijm, M., Janssens, G.E., Goh, J. and Maier, A.B. (2023), “Remote monitoring technologies for measuring cardiovascular functions in community-dwelling adults: a systematic review”, *GeroScience*, 45(5), 2939–2950. Added to strengthen recent outpatient/community wearable-monitoring coverage and the dataset-mismatch discussion. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-023-00815-4
- Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (2024), *Software and AI as a Medical Device Change Programme*. Added as current official UK regulatory context. Official URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-ai-as-a-medical-device-change-programme
- Singhal, K. et al. (2023), “Large language models encode clinical knowledge”, *Nature*, 620, 172–180. Added as peer-reviewed primary research showing both medical question-answering capability and remaining grounding, harm and bias limitations. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2
- Vasey, B. et al. (2022), “Reporting guideline for the early-stage clinical evaluation of decision support systems driven by artificial intelligence: DECIDE-AI”, *BMJ*, 377, e070904. Added to distinguish early live clinical evaluation from retrospective model and software evaluation. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2022-070904

## References removed

None. No existing reference was found to be clearly fabricated or irrelevant enough to justify removal.

## References corrected

No bibliographic record required a substantive title, author, journal or DOI correction. Access dates for new sources use 19 August 2026. Existing access dates were retained to preserve the record of the earlier review.

## Citation-quality observations

- The revised literature review now combines recent sources with foundational works instead of replacing established methods with newer commentary.
- The review distinguishes reporting guidance (TRIPOD+AI, CONSORT-AI and DECIDE-AI) from evidence that a system is clinically effective.
- Related-work comparisons remain qualitative where populations, outcomes or evaluation designs are not directly comparable.
- All URLs and DOIs in the new-reference list were verified; no DOI was inferred or generated from a title.
